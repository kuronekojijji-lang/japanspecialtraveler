Lake Kasumigaura: Cycling Around Japan's Second Largest Lake — Guide

Last updated: July 11, 2026

Lake Kasumigaura is Japan’s second-largest lake by surface area, its wide, flat horizons and traditional sailboats (once used for freshwater fishing) creating a distinctly open, peaceful landscape unlike the mountain-ringed lakes found elsewhere in Japan. A well-maintained cycling route, known as ‘Rin-Rin Road,’ circles roughly 180 km around the lake’s shoreline, passing lakeside towns, fishing harbours and rest stops, making it one of the most popular long-distance cycling routes in the Kanto region.

🗓️ At a Glance
Location Kasumigaura area, Ibaraki
Hours Always open (cycling route)
Admission Free; bicycle rental available at various points
Time needed Full day (full loop) or 1–2 hours (partial section)
Best time Spring–autumn; avoid peak summer heat

What to See

  • The roughly 180 km Rin-Rin Road cycling route.
  • Traditional sailboats, historically used for lake fishing.
  • Wide, flat horizons distinct from Japan’s mountain lakes.
  • Lakeside towns, fishing harbours and rest stops along the way.

Combine Your Visit

  • Combine with Tsuchiura sightseeing nearby.
  • Pair with Ushiku Daibutsu for a full day out.
  • Read our Ibaraki overview .

Getting There

  • By train: JR Tsuchiura Station, with bicycle rental available near the station.
  • By car: roughly 1–1.5 hours from central Tokyo, with several access points around the lake.

Tips

The full loop is a serious full-day undertaking — most visitors choose a shorter section rather than the complete circuit. Bicycle rental shops along the route often allow one-way returns at different points, useful for partial rides.
