Kanuma Autumn Festival: Carved Floats & a Curious 'Butcher's' Nickname — Guide

Last updated: July 11, 2026

The Kanuma Autumn Festival, designated a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage alongside 32 other Japanese float festivals, showcases roughly 27 elaborately carved wooden floats (yatai) built by craftsmen whose woodworking skills trace back to artisans who worked on nearby Nikko Toshogu shrine centuries ago. The festival’s English nickname, ‘Butcher’s Festival,’ is a quirky mistranslation of ‘Bukkake Matsuri’ (referring to the festival’s energetic float-pulling style), and today it draws large crowds each October to see the floats paraded through Kanuma’s streets accompanied by traditional music.

🗓️ At a Glance
Location Kanuma, Tochigi
Hours Daytime and evening, mid-October
Admission Free to view
Time needed 2–4 hours
Best time Main parade day (check exact annual dates)

What to See

  • Roughly 27 elaborately carved wooden floats (yatai).
  • Woodworking craftsmanship linked to Nikko Toshogu artisans.
  • UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage recognition.
  • Traditional music accompanying the float procession.

Combine Your Visit

  • Combine with Nikko Toshogu sightseeing to see the connected woodworking heritage.
  • Pair with Nikko Woodcarving Workshop for a hands-on craft experience.
  • Read our Tochigi overview .

Getting There

  • By train: JR Nikko Line to Kanuma Station, festival route within walking distance.
  • By car: roughly 1.5 hours from central Tokyo; expect parking restrictions on festival day.

Tips

Check the exact festival dates each year, as they can shift slightly around mid-October. The floats' intricate carvings reward a close look — take time to appreciate the craftsmanship rather than just watching the procession pass by.
