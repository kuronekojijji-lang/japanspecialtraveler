Fukuoka wears its festival culture on its sleeve. Hakata — the old merchant heart of the city — hosts two of Kyushu’s most explosive festivals within months of each other (Dontaku in May, Yamakasa in July), the region’s only Grand Sumo tournament arrives each November, and one of Japan’s biggest fireworks displays lights up the Kanmon Strait in August. Add a week-long autumn shrine festival famous for its food stalls, and Fukuoka’s 2026 calendar rewards a well-timed trip. This guide gathers the events worth planning around, with a month-by-month calendar at the end.

A note on dates: Confirmed 2026 dates are given where announced. Festival and firework dates can shift for weather or organiser decisions, and ticketed events sell out — always confirm on the official website before building a day around one.

The Great Hakata Festivals

Hakata Dontaku (博多どんたく港まつり) — May 3–4, 2026

Held on the fixed dates of May 3–4 during Golden Week, Dontaku is one of Japan’s most-attended festivals, drawing around two million people. Its roots go back some 800 years. Parades of citizens' groups march through central Hakata in costume, clapping wooden shamoji rice paddles, alongside dozens of open-air stages across the city. Free, joyous, and utterly local — but expect Golden Week crowds and book accommodation early.

Hakata Gion Yamakasa (博多祇園山笠) — July 1–15, 2026 ⭐ UNESCO heritage

Hakata’s most famous festival, a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, held July 1–15. For two weeks, ornately decorated display floats (kazari yamakasa) stand around the city, while teams train for the climax. That climax — the Oi-yama race — takes place on July 15 at 4:59 AM, when seven teams carry one-tonne kaki yamakasa floats at a sprint on a 5km course starting at Kushida Shrine. Rising before dawn to see men racing tonne-heavy floats through the streets is one of the most electric experiences in Japan. Paid seating at Kushida Shrine is balloted; the street course is free but fills fast.

Hakozaki Hojoya (筥崎宮 放生会) — September 12–18, 2026

One of Hakata’s three great festivals, held over a week at Hakozaki-gu shrine. A harvest-and-thanksgiving rite (releasing living creatures), it’s best known today for the roughly 500 food and game stalls that line the approach — one of Kyushu’s biggest and most atmospheric festival-market experiences. 2026 dates are Sept 12–18, roughly 10:00–21:00. (2026 is an off-year for the biennial grand procession.)

Grand Sumo — Kyushu Tournament (November)

Fukuoka hosts the November Grand Sumo tournament (Kyushu Basho) — the only one held on Kyushu, and the final tournament of the year. In 2026 it runs November 8–22 at the Fukuoka Kokusai Center. Fifteen days of top-division sumo; popular days sell out, so book through the official channel ahead. Arrive in the afternoon and stay for the evening’s marquee bouts.

Fireworks (Hanabi) — 2026 Dates

Fireworks display 2026 date Notes
Ashiya Fireworks (あしや花火大会) July 25 (Sat) Historic display over the Onga River mouth
Kanmon Strait Fireworks (関門海峡花火大会), 39th August 13 (Thu), 20:00–20:30 ~18,000 shells across both banks (Moji, Kitakyushu & Shimonoseki) — Japan’s only fireworks festival spanning a prefectural border. A 900-drone “Strait Drone Show” debuts on the Shimonoseki side in 2026

Tip: The Kanmon Strait display is spectacular but extremely crowded on both sides — arrive very early, use public transport, and expect long post-show queues. Moji-ko Retro makes a scenic base.

Pop Culture & Seasonal Events

  • Fukuoka’s museum & pop-culture scene — the Fukuoka Art Museum and Fukuoka Asian Art Museum rotate major exhibitions through 2026; check current shows for your dates.
  • Kitakyushu Pop Culture — Kitakyushu hosts anime and manga events at venues like West Japan General Exhibition Center; the city is a filming and pop-culture hub. Check event calendars.
  • Nakasu & yatai season — Fukuoka’s famous yatai food stalls operate year-round along Nakasu and Tenjin; not an “event,” but a nightly one worth planning an evening around.

Cherry Blossom Season (Spring 2026)

Fukuoka’s blossoms peak in late March to early April.

  • Maizuru Park & Fukuoka Castle ruins — the city’s premier hanami site, with evening light-ups
  • Nishi Park — hilltop cherries with harbour views
  • Nokonoshima Island Park — flowers and sea views a short ferry from the city

Autumn Leaves (Nov 2026)

Peaks mid-to-late November in the hills around the city.

  • Akizuki — a former castle town with a famous maple-lined approach
  • Raizan Sennyoji Daihioin — a mountain temple renowned for its foliage
  • Kamado Shrine (Dazaifu) and the Dazaifu Tenmangu grounds

Winter Illuminations (2025–2026 season, and again from late 2026)

  • Hakata Hikari no Machi — the large display around JR Hakata Station and Hakata Station Square
  • Fukuoka Tower — the tower lit as a seasonal “illumination tower”
  • Mojiko Retro (Kitakyushu) — the historic port district illuminated for winter

New Year in Fukuoka (Late Dec – Early Jan)

  • Hatsumode at Dazaifu Tenmangu (dedicated to the deity of learning — hugely popular with students) and Kushida Shrine
  • Tamaseseri (Jan 3) at Hakozaki-gu — a dramatic “ball-catching” contest between two teams for the year’s fortune
  • New Year countdowns around Tenjin and Canal City

Practical Tips

  • Book far ahead for Golden Week and mid-July. Dontaku (May 3–4) and Yamakasa’s climax (July 15) are Fukuoka’s peak dates — hotels fill and prices climb months in advance.
  • Yamakasa means an early start. The Oi-yama race is at 4:59 AM on July 15 — plan your night around it, or watch a nagare-gaki practice run in the days before.
  • Confirm every date on the official site. Firework and festival dates move for weather; ticketed events (sumo, Yamakasa balloted seats) sell out.
  • Kanmon fireworks logistics. Both banks are packed — arrive very early, go by train, and be ready for long queues afterward.
  • Summer heat. July–August events are hot and humid; water, a hat, and a fan are essentials.
