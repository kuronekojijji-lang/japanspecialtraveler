The Kako River Wetlands: Birds and Riverside Paths — Guide

The Kako River is the longest river in Hyogo, and where it widens toward the Seto Inland Sea at Kakogawa it becomes something quietly rewarding: a broad, shallow, braided channel of reed beds, gravel bars and shifting sandbanks. Herons and egrets stalk the shallows all year, and autumn and winter bring ducks, waders and other migrants down onto the open water. Level, well-surfaced paths run for kilometres along both banks, making this one of the easiest places in the prefecture for an unhurried walk or a flat cycle ride with a hire bike.

🗓️ At a Glance
Location Kakogawa, Hyogo
Hours Always open
Admission Free
Time needed 1–3 hours
Best time Oct–Mar for migrating waterfowl

What to Do

  • Herons and egrets stalking the shallows year-round.
  • Migrating ducks and waders from autumn through winter.
  • Reed beds, gravel bars and braided shallow channels.
  • Level riverside paths for walking and flat cycling.

Combine Your Visit

  • Combine with Himeji Castle a short ride west.
  • Pair with Sakoshi further along the coast.
  • Read our Hyogo overview .

Getting There

  • By train: JR Kakogawa Station, with the river a few minutes' walk away.
  • By bike: the riverside paths are the best way to cover distance.

Tips

This is an everyday local landscape rather than a sight, so come for the walking and the birds rather than expecting facilities — bring binoculars, water and a hat, as the banks are open and shadeless. October to March is much the best window, when migrating waterfowl are on the water; early morning gives the calmest conditions and the most activity.
