Yamaguchi Events 2026: Lantern Festival, Kanmon Strait Fireworks & Summer Matsuri

Yamaguchi, the prefecture at the far western tip of Honshu, blends samurai history, dramatic coastlines and one of Japan’s most atmospheric festivals. Here you’ll find the arch-stone Kintaikyo Bridge, the cliff-top red torii of Motonosumi Inari, the vast Akiyoshido cave, the old castle town of Hagi — and, each August, a 600-year-old festival that fills the streets of Yamaguchi City with tens of thousands of glowing red lanterns. Its 2026 event calendar peaks in summer, with a famous lantern night and one of Japan’s biggest fireworks displays over the Kanmon Strait. This guide gathers the events worth planning a 2026 trip around, with a month-by-month calendar at the end.

A note on dates: Confirmed 2026 dates are given where announced. Festival and firework dates can shift for weather or organiser decisions, and ticketed seating sells out — always confirm on the official website before building a day around one.

Yamaguchi Tanabata Chochin Festival (山口七夕ちょうちんまつり) — August 6–7, 2026 ⭐

The signature event of the prefecture and one of Yamaguchi’s three great summer festivals. Held in central Yamaguchi City on August 6–7, 2026 (roughly 18:30–21:30), this festival dates back around 600 years to the Muromachi period, when the ruling Ouchi clan introduced it. Tall bamboo poles are hung with clusters of red paper lanterns — tens of thousands of them across the city centre — and each flame is lit by hand, one lantern at a time. The shopping arcades, the Yamaguchi Station approach, Park Road and the Yuda Onsen area glow crimson after dark. It is a quieter, more traditional counterpart to the big drum-and-dance festivals elsewhere in Japan, and utterly photogenic. Combine it with an overnight stay at Yuda Onsen next door.

Kanmon Strait Fireworks (関門海峡花火大会) — August 13, 2026

One of the largest fireworks displays in western Japan, fired every August 13 from both shores of the Kanmon Strait — the Shimonoseki side in Yamaguchi and the Moji side in Kitakyushu (Fukuoka) — so around 18,000 shells burst over the water from two directions at once. The 2026 programme is set to include a 40-shell barrage of isshakudama (30 cm shells), underwater fireworks, the recurring “Phoenix” recovery-prayer finale, and an 800-drone strait show. The waterfront fills early and paid seating sells out; the Shimonoseki side pairs well with a visit to Karato Market and the Kanmon area.

Nishikigawa Water Festival & Kintaikyo Fireworks (錦川水の祭典) — August 2026

Iwakuni marks August as its “Nishikigawa Water Festival” month, with short nightly firework bursts (around 75 shells over about two minutes) staged across roughly a dozen evenings above the Nishiki River beside the famous five-arch Kintaikyo Bridge. It’s a low-key, repeatable alternative to the big one-night displays — check Iwakuni’s schedule for the 2026 dates and the main festival night.

Shimonoseki Kaikyo Matsuri (下関海峡まつり) — early May 2026

Held over the early-May holidays (traditionally around May 2–4), this Shimonoseki festival centres on the Sentei-sai rites at Akama Shrine and a colourful re-enactment linked to the Battle of Dan-no-ura, the decisive 1185 sea battle of the Genpei War fought in these very straits. Costumed processions, courtesan parades and a lively harbour atmosphere. Confirm 2026 dates locally.

Cherry Blossom Season (Spring 2026)

Yamaguchi’s blossoms peak in late March to early April.

  • Tokiwa Park (Ube) — a huge lakeside park famous for cherries and sculpture
  • Kintaikyo Bridge (Iwakuni) — cherries framing the arched wooden bridge, one of Japan’s classic spring scenes
  • Hagi Castle ruins (Shizuki Park) — blossoms among the old samurai town’s stone walls

Autumn Leaves (Nov 2026)

  • Kintaikyo Bridge & Momijidani Park (Iwakuni) — maples beside the arches, superb in mid-to-late November
  • Akiyoshidai plateau — the karst grasslands turn golden as the pampas grass ripens
  • Choanji Temple (Shimonoseki) and Ruriko-ji five-storey pagoda (Yamaguchi) framed in red

Winter & New Year

  • Motonosumi Inari Shrine — 123 red torii tumbling down to the Japan Sea cliffs, dramatic under winter light
  • Fugu (pufferfish) season peaks — Shimonoseki is Japan’s fugu capital; Karato Market is the place to try it
  • Hatsumode at Ruriko-ji and the prefecture’s shrines; Akiyoshido cave stays a mild 17°C year-round

Practical Tips

  • Book Yamaguchi City ahead for August 6–7. The lantern festival fills the centre and nearby Yuda Onsen — reserve a room early and plan to arrive before dusk when the lanterns are lit.
  • The Kanmon fireworks span two prefectures. You can watch the August 13 display from either the Shimonoseki (Yamaguchi) or Moji (Fukuoka) side; both sell paid seating and both get very crowded — decide your side and transport in advance.
  • Confirm every date on the official site. Lantern-festival, fireworks and Kaikyo Matsuri dates should be checked each year before you travel.
  • Yamaguchi is spread out. Shimonoseki, Yamaguchi City, Hagi and Iwakuni are far apart — a rental car or careful train planning helps you combine events with the coast and caves.
  • Summer heat. August events are hot and humid; carry water, a hat and a fan.
