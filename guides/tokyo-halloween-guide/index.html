Shibuya Halloween: What's Really Happening Now (Honest Guide)

Last updated: June 20, 2026

For years, Shibuya’s Halloween was famous as one of the world’s largest spontaneous costume gatherings — tens of thousands of revellers in elaborate outfits flooding the scramble crossing with no organiser, no tickets, just an enormous self-generating street party. If you’ve read older travel guides, that’s the image they sell.

Here’s the honest, current reality: Shibuya no longer wants this, and is actively asking people — especially crowds and tourists — to stay away on Halloween. This guide gives you the straight facts on what’s changed, why, what to expect if you go anyway, and — more usefully — the genuinely better places to enjoy Halloween in Tokyo.

⚠️ The Situation: Shibuya Is Discouraging Halloween Crowds

After years of escalating overcrowding, property damage, injuries, and a fatal crowd crush in Seoul’s Itaewon (2022) that alarmed authorities worldwide, Shibuya Ward has shifted from tolerating its Halloween to actively discouraging it. In recent years the city has:

  • Publicly told visitors NOT to come to Shibuya for Halloween — the mayor has issued direct “please don’t come” messages aimed at tourists and partygoers.
  • Banned street drinking in the Shibuya Station area during the Halloween period (and now year-round in the central area during set evening hours), with signage in multiple languages.
  • Asked convenience stores and shops near the station to stop selling alcohol during Halloween nights.
  • Deployed large numbers of police and security guards, closed or restricted areas, covered the famous Hachiko statue, and managed pedestrian flow to prevent dangerous crowding.

The message is unambiguous: Shibuya is no longer hosting a Halloween party, and turning up expecting one is discouraged and increasingly difficult.

What This Means for You

If you go to Shibuya on Halloween expecting the legendary street party, you’ll likely find:

  • Heavy police presence and barriers
  • No street drinking (and possibly no alcohol sales nearby)
  • Crowd-control measures funnelling people through
  • An official atmosphere of “move along,” not celebration

Our honest recommendation: don’t plan your Halloween around Shibuya. It’s not the experience it once was, it goes against the express wishes of the local community, and the crowd-safety concerns are real. Respecting the city’s request is the responsible thing to do as a visitor.

Better Places to Enjoy Halloween in Tokyo

The good news: Tokyo has many fun, welcoming, and safe Halloween experiences. Here’s where to actually go.

1. Tokyo Disney Resort (Disneyland & DisneySea)

Disney runs one of the best Halloween seasons anywhere — weeks of elaborate decorations, special parades and shows, themed food and merchandise, and (on select days) guests in costume. It’s organised, festive, and genuinely magical. See our DisneySea guide . Book dated tickets early — Halloween season is popular.

2. Universal Studios Japan (Osaka — for the dedicated)

If you’re travelling beyond Tokyo, USJ’s “Halloween Horror Nights” is Japan’s most famous scare event. A day trip / overnight from Tokyo for horror fans.

3. Theme Parks & Attractions Near Tokyo

Yomiuriland, Sanrio Puroland, and other parks run family-friendly Halloween events with decorations, shows, and trick-or-treating — great for families.

4. Roppongi & Organised Club/Bar Events

For nightlife-oriented Halloween, Roppongi and Shinjuku host organised, ticketed Halloween parties at clubs and venues — a costume night with an actual host, security, and a defined space (rather than an unmanaged street crush). Buy tickets in advance.

5. Kawasaki Halloween Parade

Just south of Tokyo, the Kawasaki Halloween Parade is a long-running, organised costume parade with judged categories and a genuine festival atmosphere — the proper, sanctioned alternative to Shibuya’s chaos for those who love dressing up. Check dates and any participation/registration rules.

6. Themed Cafés & Seasonal Pop-ups

Tokyo’s cafés, including the maid cafés of Akihabara and countless character cafés, run Halloween menus and decorations throughout October — a low-key, fun way to mark the season.

If You Still Want to See Shibuya

If you simply want to visit Shibuya around Halloween to see the crossing and the atmosphere (not to party):

  • Go earlier in October, not on the 31st, to enjoy the decorated shops and seasonal mood without the discouraged crowds.
  • Visit during the day rather than night.
  • Follow all signage and police direction, don’t drink in the street, and don’t add to the crowding.
  • Consider seeing the crossing from above instead — see our Shibuya guide for viewpoints like Shibuya Sky and Mag’s Park.

The Bottom Line

The Shibuya Halloween of the old travel guides is effectively over — by the deliberate choice of the city and its residents, for safety reasons. As a visitor, the respectful and genuinely more enjoyable move is to celebrate Halloween elsewhere in Tokyo, where it’s welcomed. Tokyo loves Halloween — just not as an unmanaged crush at the scramble crossing.

Getting Around

For the recommended alternatives:

  • Tokyo Disney Resort: JR Keiyo Line to Maihama Station.
  • Roppongi nightlife: Tokyo Metro Hibiya/Oedo Line to Roppongi.
  • Kawasaki Parade: JR to Kawasaki Station (south of Tokyo).
  • Sanrio Puroland: Keio/Odakyu line to Tama-Center.
