Osaka does festivals the way it does everything — loud, warm, and food-first. The city’s 2026 calendar runs from one of Japan’s three great festivals (Tenjin Matsuri, with its river-boat procession and fireworks) to the heart-stopping cart races of Kishiwada Danjiri, a spring Grand Sumo tournament, the year’s biggest anime collaborations at Universal Studios Japan, and a cherry-blossom tunnel opened for just one week. This guide gathers the events worth planning a 2026 trip around, with a month-by-month calendar at the end.

A note on dates: Confirmed 2026 dates are given where announced. Festival and firework dates can shift for weather or organiser decisions, and ticketed events sell out — always confirm on the official website before building a day around one.

Summer Fireworks (Hanabi) — 2026 Dates

Fireworks display 2026 date Notes
Tenjin Matsuri Hono Fireworks (天神祭奉納花火) July 25 (Sat), 19:30–20:50 ~3,000 shells over the Okawa river, launched from Kawasaki Park (by the Mint) and Sakuranomiya Park, alongside the festival’s lantern-lit boat flotilla
Naniwa Yodogawa Fireworks (なにわ淀川花火大会), 38th October 17 (Sat), 19:30–20:00 One of the Kansai region’s biggest; held on the Yodogawa riverbed. Note the autumn (October) timing

Tips: The Tenjin fireworks are best seen as part of the whole festival evening (see below). For the Yodogawa display, riverside spots fill early and paid seating (including Umeda Sky Building viewing plans) is the easiest way to guarantee a view. Summer displays are hot and humid — bring water and a fan.

Traditional Festivals (Matsuri)

Osaka’s summer is defined by its three great festivals — the Aizen Matsuri opens the season, the Tenjin Matsuri is the centrepiece, and the Sumiyoshi Matsuri closes it. Then autumn brings the most thrilling festival of all.

Aizen Matsuri (愛染まつり) — June 30 – July 2, 2026

The festival that traditionally opens Osaka’s summer, held at Shomanin Aizendo temple. Best known for the Aizen Musume (festival maidens) riding decorated hoekago palanquins, plus food stalls and a lively, local atmosphere. Runs roughly 10:00 to 21:00 across three days.

Tenjin Matsuri (天神祭) — July 24–25, 2026 ⭐ One of Japan’s 3 great festivals

The grand festival of Osaka Tenmangu shrine, honouring Sugawara no Michizane, deity of learning — and one of Japan’s three greatest festivals alongside Kyoto’s Gion Matsuri and Tokyo’s Kanda Matsuri. July 24 is the land procession (Rikutogyo); July 25 is the spectacular Funatogyo river procession, when around a hundred boats carry the deity along the Okawa river by lantern light, culminating in the Hono fireworks. It draws well over a million people. The riverside on the evening of the 25th is the single best festival experience in Osaka.

Sumiyoshi Matsuri (住吉祭) — late July / around Aug 1, 2026

The festival that closes Osaka’s summer, held at Sumiyoshi Taisha, one of Japan’s oldest shrines. The highlight is the mikoshi togyo, when a heavy portable shrine is carried across the Yamato River — a purification rite for the whole city. Confirm exact 2026 dates on the shrine’s site.

Kishiwada Danjiri Matsuri (岸和田だんじり祭) — Sept 19–20 & Oct 10–11, 2026

Osaka’s most exhilarating — and genuinely dangerous — festival. Teams sprint through the streets of Kishiwada hauling multi-tonne wooden danjiri carts, taking corners at full speed in a manoeuvre called yarimawashi while a leader dances on the roof. The September festival (Sep 19–20) is the famous one; a second October festival (Oct 10–11) covers other districts. Arrive early for a safe viewing spot behind the barriers and follow all steward instructions.

Namba Yasaka Lion Dance (難波八阪神社) — January

The giant lion-head stage of Namba Yasaka Shrine hosts a New-Year lion dance (shishimai) in mid-January, believed to swallow bad luck. A striking, photogenic stop early in the year.

Grand Sumo — March Tournament (Osaka)

Osaka hosts the March Grand Sumo tournament (Haru-basho) — one of only three held outside Tokyo. In 2026 it runs March 8–22 at Edion Arena Osaka (Osaka Prefectural Gymnasium) in Namba. Fifteen days of top-level sumo, from lower-division bouts in the early afternoon to the marquee matches around 5–6pm. Popular days sell out — book through the official channel well ahead. It’s the only chance to see a full honbasho in western Japan.

Pop Culture & One-Off 2026 Events

Universal Cool Japan 2026 — USJ, from January 30, 2026

Universal Studios Japan’s headline annual event brings the year’s biggest anime and game collaborations to the park. Universal Cool Japan 2026 (from Jan 30) unites five major titles: Detective Conan, Jujutsu Kaisen, Frieren: Beyond Journey’s End, the Masquerade film series (from Keigo Higashino’s novels), and Monster Hunter Wilds — with limited attractions, food, and merchandise found nowhere else. A new walkthrough attraction for Frieren is scheduled from May 30, 2026 for a limited run. USJ also runs its seasonal programmes across the year — spring, the summer “cool” water events, Halloween Horror Nights in autumn, and Christmas — so check the park calendar for your dates.

teamLab Botanical Garden Osaka & seasonal exhibitions

Osaka’s digital-art draw, teamLab Botanical Garden Osaka at Nagai Park, runs its immersive night walk year-round with seasonal changes. The city’s museums (Nakanoshima Museum of Art, the National Museum of Art, Osaka) rotate major exhibitions through 2026 — check current shows for your travel window.

Cherry Blossom Season (Spring 2026)

Mint Bureau “Cherry Blossom Tunnel” (造幣局 桜の通り抜け) — April 9–15, 2026

Osaka’s signature spring event. For just seven days, the Japan Mint opens its 560-metre riverside path lined with around 340 trees of some 140 varieties — many of them rare late-blooming double cherries you won’t see elsewhere. 2026 dates are April 9–15, with advance online reservation required (first-come; applications open March 18). Going in the evening, when the blossoms are lit, is the classic experience.

Other top hanami spots: Osaka Castle Park (Nishinomaru Garden), Kema Sakuranomiya Park along the Okawa river, and Expo ‘70 Commemorative Park in Suita.

Autumn Leaves (Late Nov)

Osaka’s foliage peaks in late November. Best spots: Minoo Park (a waterfall trail famous for maple leaves — and deep-fried maple-leaf tempura), Osaka Castle Park, and the temple gardens of nearby areas. The Naniwa Yodogawa Fireworks on Oct 17 make an unusual autumn pairing.

Winter Illuminations (2025–2026 season, and again from late 2026)

  • Osaka Festival of Lights (Osaka Hikari no Kyoen) — the umbrella winter light event, including the Midosuji Illumination (2026 edition: Nov 3 – Dec 31) lining the city’s main boulevard, and the Osaka Hikari-Renaissance on Nakanoshima
  • Osaka Castle Illuminage — light displays in Nishinomaru Garden (Nov 1, 2025 – Feb 1, 2026)
  • Department-store and bay-area displays citywide

New Year in Osaka (Late Dec – Early Jan)

  • Hatsumode at Sumiyoshi Taisha (one of Japan’s busiest, with over two million visitors) and Osaka Tenmangu
  • Toka Ebisu (Jan 9–11) at Imamiya Ebisu Shrine — a huge business-fortune festival where crowds seek lucky bamboo branches
  • New Year countdowns and sales across Namba and Umeda

Practical Tips

  • Book early for peak dates. Hotels around Tenjin Matsuri (late July), the March sumo, and the cherry season fill and rise in price months ahead.
  • Confirm every date on the official site. Firework and festival dates move for weather; ticketed events (sumo, USJ, Mint tunnel reservations) sell out.
  • Kishiwada Danjiri safety. The carts move fast and accidents happen — stay behind barriers, keep children close, and follow steward directions.
  • Tenjin Matsuri evening. For the July 25 river procession and fireworks, claim a riverside spot hours ahead, or book a paid viewing plan.
  • Summer heat. July–August events are hot and humid; water, a hat, and a hand fan are essentials.
