Katsuura Katsuo (Bonito) Guide: What to Eat & Access

Last updated: June 20, 2026

The Boso port of Katsuura lands some of Japan’s finest katsuo (skipjack bonito) — a fish so central to the town that it even shares its name (katsuo-ura). Riding the warm Kuroshio current, fat spring “first bonito” (hatsu-gatsuo) and rich autumn “returning bonito” (modori-gatsuo) come ashore here at their peak. The classic way to enjoy it is katsuo no tataki — fillets seared over straw so the outside is smoky and the inside stays ruby-rare — served with ginger, garlic, and ponzu. Eaten fresh by the harbour with the morning market nearby, it’s one of Chiba’s great seafood experiences.

This guide covers the tataki, the season, how to eat it and access.

🗓️ Quick Reference
Location Katsuura, Boso Peninsula, Chiba
Star Katsuo (skipjack bonito)
Signature dish Katsuo no tataki (seared bonito)
Season Spring & autumn bonito runs
Good for Seafood lovers, coastal day trips

The katsuo

  • Katsuura is a major bonito landing port, fed by the warm Kuroshio current.
  • Hatsu-gatsuo (first bonito) in spring is lean and fresh; modori-gatsuo (returning bonito) in autumn is fatty and rich.
  • Enjoy it as tataki (straw-seared), sashimi, or in rice bowls.

How to eat it

  • Katsuo no tataki: seared outside, raw inside, sliced thick and topped with grated ginger, garlic, scallion and ponzu.
  • Pair with local sake or a cold beer by the harbour.
  • Many shops near the morning market serve it fresh — see our Katsuura sashimi guide .

Tips

  • Visit during the spring or autumn runs for peak flavour.
  • Combine with the morning market for the freshest catch.

Combine your visit

Katsuo rounds out a Katsuura day:

  • Browse the historic morning market for sashimi — see our Katsuura sashimi guide .
  • Time it with the Big Hina Matsuri doll festival — see our Katsuura Hina Matsuri guide .

How to get there

  • JR Sotobo line to Katsuura Station, then walk to the harbour and market diners.
  • ~1.5–2 hours from Tokyo Station by limited express.
