Just north of Tokyo, Saitama is an easy day trip that hides two of Japan’s most important festivals. The Chichibu Night Festival and the Kawagoe Matsuri are both UNESCO-recognised float processions, and the prefecture also stages a Guinness-record firework, a hillside of half a million moss-phlox blooms, and the “Little Edo” charm of Kawagoe’s warehouse district. This guide gathers the events worth planning a 2026 trip around, with a month-by-month calendar at the end.

A note on dates: Confirmed 2026 dates are given where announced. Festival and firework dates can shift for weather or organiser decisions, and ticketed events sell out — always confirm on the official website before building a day around one.

The Two UNESCO Float Festivals

Chichibu Night Festival (秩父夜祭) — December 2–3, 2026 ⭐

One of Japan’s three great float festivals (with Kyoto’s Gion and Takayama’s), and one of the very few winter festivals with a major fireworks display. Held on the fixed dates of December 2 (eve) and December 3 (main festival), it sends enormous illuminated kasaboko floats — some weighing up to 15 tonnes — hauled up a slope by teams to the sound of drums and flutes, while fireworks light the cold night sky from about 19:30 to 22:00. The combination of lantern-lit floats and winter fireworks is unforgettable. Dress very warmly and expect big crowds; paid seating is available.

Kawagoe Matsuri (川越まつり) — mid-October 2026

Kawagoe’s UNESCO-inscribed autumn festival transforms the “Little Edo” warehouse district into a stage for towering, ornately carved floats topped with figures of gods and legends. The dramatic highlight is the hikkawase — floats meeting at intersections in a face-off of music and lanterns. Traditionally held on the third weekend of October (2026: around October 17–18) — confirm the exact dates on the official site. Combine with a stroll through Kawagoe’s Kurazukuri street and Candy Alley.

Fireworks (Hanabi) — 2026 Dates

Fireworks display 2026 date Notes
Saitama City Fireworks (さいたま市花火大会) late July (from Jul 25) Multiple neighbourhood displays including Owada Park (Omiya)
Kumagaya Fireworks (熊谷花火大会), 74th August 8 (Sat), 19:00–21:00 A major display on the Arakawa riverbank
Kounosu Fireworks (こうのす花火大会) October 10 (Sat) Home of the yonshakudama — the Guinness-record “world’s heaviest firework shell”
Koedo Kawagoe Fireworks (小江戸川越花火大会) November 21 (Sat) A rarer autumn display

Tip: The Kounosu display in October is the one for firework enthusiasts — the four-shaku shell is a once-seen-never-forgotten spectacle. Book paid seating for the best view.

Hitsujiyama Shibazakura (羊山公園 芝桜) — April 3 – May 6, 2026

On the slopes below Mt. Buko in Chichibu, the Hitsujiyama Park “Hill of Shibazakura” bursts into a patchwork of over 400,000 moss-phlox plants in pink, white, and purple across roughly 17,600㎡. 2026 dates are April 3 – May 6 (8:00–17:00), overlapping cherry-blossom and Golden Week travel. A Chichibu market, pony rides, and sheep-shearing demonstrations run during the festival. About 20 minutes' walk from Seibu-Chichibu Station.

Cherry Blossom Season (Spring 2026)

Saitama’s blossoms peak in late March to early April.

  • Omiya Park (Saitama City) — hundreds of cherry trees, one of the Kanto region’s classic hanami spots
  • Kumagaya Sakura-tsutsumi — a long riverside avenue of cherries
  • Gongendo (Satte) — cherries above a valley of golden canola flowers, a striking springtime combination

Pop Culture & Family Days Out

  • Moominvalley Park (Hanno) — the Moomin theme park at Metsä Village runs seasonal events year-round
  • Tokorozawa Sakura Town / EJ Anime Hotel — a pop-culture and anime complex (KADOKAWA) with rotating exhibitions
  • Railway Museum (Omiya) — one of Japan’s best for train fans, with seasonal programmes
  • Seibuen and other family parks stage seasonal illuminations and events

Autumn Leaves (Nov 2026)

Chichibu and the western hills lead Saitama’s autumn, peaking mid-to-late November.

  • Chichibu / Nagatoro — the Iwadatami rocks and gorge, with maple light-ups at Tsukinoishi Momiji Park
  • Kawagoe temple gardens and Heirin-ji (Niiza)

Winter Illuminations & New Year

  • Tokorozawa & Seibuen winter illuminations run into early 2026
  • Hatsumode at Hikawa Shrine (Omiya) — the head of some 280 Hikawa shrines nationwide — and Kawagoe Hikawa Shrine
  • Kawagoe Hikawa Shrine’s summer wind-chime (furin) corridor (July–Sept) is a hugely photogenic seasonal installation

Practical Tips

  • Book ahead for December and mid-October. The Chichibu Night Festival (Dec 2–3) and Kawagoe Matsuri are Saitama’s peak dates — nearby accommodation fills early.
  • Confirm every date on the official site. Firework and festival dates move for weather; the Kawagoe Matsuri weekend and paid seating should be checked.
  • Dress for winter at Chichibu. The December festival is genuinely cold at night — proper layers are essential for the fireworks.
  • Saitama is an easy day trip. Kawagoe, Omiya, Chichibu, and Kounosu are all reachable from central Tokyo in under 1.5 hours.
  • Summer heat. July–August events are hot and humid; water, a hat, and a fan are essentials.
