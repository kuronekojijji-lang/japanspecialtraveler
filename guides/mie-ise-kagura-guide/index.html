Ise Jingu Kagura: Sacred Music and Dance at Ise — Guide

Last updated: July 17, 2026

Beyond the quiet act of prayer, Ise Jingu preserves kagura — formal ceremonies of sacred music and dance offered to the deities on a worshipper’s behalf. Performed in the kaguraden halls of both the Naiku and Geku by shrine musicians in courtly costume and miko shrine maidens, these rituals set ancient gagaku court music and slow, stylised dance to convey prayers for health, gratitude or family blessing. Anyone can request a kagura offering for a fee, making it a rare opportunity to witness — and personally commission — living ceremonial tradition at the heart of Japan’s most sacred shrine.

🗓️ At a Glance
Location Ise Jingu (Naiku & Geku), Mie
Hours Daytime, during shrine hours
Admission Kagura offerings from ~¥5,000
Time needed 25–40 minutes
Best time Year-round

What to See

  • Formal kagura ceremonies of sacred music and dance.
  • Ancient gagaku court music and miko shrine-maiden dance.
  • The kaguraden ceremonial halls of Naiku and Geku.
  • A prayer offering personally commissioned to the gods.

Combine Your Visit

  • Combine with the Naiku and Geku shrines.
  • Pair with Okage Yokocho afterwards.
  • Read our Mie overview .

Getting There

  • At the shrines: apply at the kaguraden reception at either Naiku or Geku.
  • Fees: offerings are tiered; higher tiers add more dancers and music.

Tips

You don’t need to be Shinto to request a kagura — visitors are welcome to commission one, and it’s a moving way to experience the shrine beyond simply walking through. Apply at the kaguraden reception; the fee tiers determine the scale, from a modest offering to a full performance with multiple dancers, and staff will guide first-timers through the simple etiquette.
