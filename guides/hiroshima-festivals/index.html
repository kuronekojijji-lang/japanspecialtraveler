Hiroshima’s event calendar is unusually wide in tone. The August 6 Peace Memorial Ceremony is one of the world’s most significant annual commemorations; the Kangensai boat festival on Miyajima is one of Japan’s oldest and most atmospheric Shinto rituals; the Flower Festival draws over a million people during Golden Week. Add summer fireworks over the bay, a legendary sake festival, and a beloved baseball team, and Hiroshima rewards a well-timed 2026 trip. This guide gathers the events worth planning around, with a month-by-month calendar at the end.

A note on dates: Confirmed 2026 dates are given where announced. Festival and firework dates can shift for weather or organiser decisions, and ticketed events sell out — always confirm on the official website before building a day around one.

Peace Memorial Ceremony — August 6, 2026

The most important event in Hiroshima’s calendar, held every August 6 in Peace Memorial Park to commemorate the victims of the atomic bombing.

  • Time: 08:00–08:45 (the Peace Bell rings at 08:15 — the exact moment of the bomb)
  • Location: Cenotaph for the A-Bomb Victims, Peace Memorial Park
  • Attendance: Free, no reservation; arrive by 07:30 for a viewing spot

The ceremony features the Mayor’s Peace Declaration, dove releases, and choral performances by schoolchildren, with heads of state and ambassadors from over 100 countries attending. On the evening of the 6th, the Tōrō Nagashi lantern-floating ceremony releases thousands of paper lanterns onto the Motoyasu River (register a lantern for ¥1,000). This is a place of mourning — keep voices low and dress modestly.

Hiroshima Flower Festival — May 3–5, 2026

The city’s biggest non-memorial event, held on the fixed dates of May 3–5 during Golden Week. Three days of parades, taiko competitions, dance performances, and food stalls fill Peace Boulevard and the area around Peace Park, drawing around 1.5 million visitors. Free; arrive well before main events for a spot along the parade route.

Miyajima Kangensai (管絃祭) — July 30, 2026 ⭐

One of Japan’s three great boat festivals, a 12th-century imperial-style ceremony held on the 17th day of the 6th lunar month — July 30 in 2026. A portable shrine carrying the deities of Itsukushima Shrine is rowed across Miyajima’s harbour and around the floating torii gate by a flotilla of decorated boats, to traditional gagaku court music, with torchlight after dark. Solemn and mesmerising — the music drifting across the water at twilight defines a trip. Free viewing from the shrine shore; arrive two hours ahead.

Note: The famous Miyajima Underwater Fireworks returned in 2025 after a long hiatus — check whether a 2026 edition is confirmed before planning around it.

Hiroshima Toyo Carp Baseball (late Mar – early Oct)

The Hiroshima Toyo Carp are the city’s beloved, citizen-owned baseball team, and a home game at Mazda Stadium is one of the best evening experiences for visitors — coordinated chants, a 7th-inning balloon release, red-jersey crowds, and oyster and beer stalls. Tickets from ¥2,000; buy on the official site. Wear red.

Other Major Festivals

Toukasan Yukata Festival (とうかさん) — early June

Hiroshima City’s traditional summer kickoff — three days of yukata-clad crowds at Enryuji Temple, with around 450,000 attendees. Held on the first Friday–Sunday of June. The best time to try on a yukata (rental shops nearby).

Saijo Sake Festival (西条酒まつり) — October 10–11, 2026

The town of Saijo, 40 minutes east of Hiroshima, is one of Japan’s three great sake-brewing centres. 2026 dates are October 10–11 (10:00–19:00 on the 10th, 10:00–17:00 on the 11th), when the streets fill with tasting booths from over 1,000 breweries nationwide (~¥1,800 for the unlimited tasting badge). Draws around 250,000 visitors.

Onomichi Betcha Festival (ベッチャー祭り) — November 1–3, 2026

A 200-year-old Onomichi festival where masked dancers chase children through the streets, tapping them with sticks for good luck. Strange, lively, and uniquely local.

Seasonal Highlights

Cherry Blossoms (late March – early April)

  • Hiroshima Castle moat — cherries lit at night
  • Peace Memorial Park — riverside cherries with the A-Bomb Dome behind
  • Miyajima and Senkoji Park (Onomichi) — blossoms with sea and torii views

Autumn Leaves (mid–late November)

  • Momijidani Park (Miyajima) — the prefecture’s most famous maple site
  • Taishakukyo Gorge and Sandankyo — dramatic gorge foliage
  • Shukkeien Garden — evening illuminations

Winter Illuminations

  • Dreamination on Heiwa-Odori Boulevard (mid-November to early January, free)
  • Miyajima — Itsukushima Shrine floodlit from sunset to 23:00 year-round

Practical Tips

  • Book very early for August 6. Hotels around the Peace Ceremony fill six or more months ahead; Golden Week (Flower Festival) roughly doubles rates.
  • Confirm every date on the official site. Firework and festival dates move for weather; Kangensai follows the lunar calendar, and ticketed events sell out.
  • August is extremely hot. Water, electrolytes, a hat, and a fan are essentials for the ceremony and summer festivals.
  • Respectful behaviour at the Peace Park. During the ceremony, keep quiet, dress modestly, and avoid cheerful tourist photos at the cenotaph.
  • Combine Miyajima and the city. Kangensai (Miyajima) and the Peace Park sit within an easy day of each other by tram and ferry.
