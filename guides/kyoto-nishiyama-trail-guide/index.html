Nishiyama Trail Guide: Kyoto's Quiet Western Mountains

Most visitors never set foot in Nishiyama — the wooded western mountains of Kyoto — and that is exactly its appeal. While the eastern hills heave with tour groups, this quiet range of bamboo forests, hillside temples, and country lanes offers some of the city’s most peaceful temple-going and gentle hiking, crowned by the sprawling mountainside temple of Yoshimine-dera with its sweeping views over the Kyoto basin. For travellers who have already seen the headline sights and want the Kyoto that locals keep for themselves, Nishiyama is a rewarding escape.

This guide covers the temples and trails of western Kyoto, the best season, and how to reach an area that rewards a little extra effort.

🗓️ Quick Reference
Location Western Kyoto (Nishiyama), Nishikyo-ku & beyond
Highlights Yoshimine-dera, Komyo-ji, Oharano Shrine
Best season Autumn (foliage), spring (fresh green)
Crowds Low — a genuine off-the-beaten-path area
Effort Some hill walking; buses less frequent

Yoshimine-dera: The Mountainside Temple

The jewel of Nishiyama, Yoshimine-dera is a large temple complex climbing the western mountains, founded in 1029. Its halls, pagoda, and gardens are spread across the hillside connected by stone paths, and from its upper levels you get a magnificent panorama over the whole Kyoto basin. It’s famous for an extraordinary ancient pine, the Yuryu-no-matsu, trained horizontally over many metres, and for spectacular autumn foliage that draws those in the know. Wandering its terraced grounds, you’ll often have the views almost to yourself.

Other Nishiyama Temples & Shrines

The western hills hide a cluster of beautiful, little-visited sites:

  • Komyo-ji: Famous for a “maple corridor” — a tunnel of maples that turns brilliant red in autumn — and an approach of great beauty, yet far quieter than the eastern foliage temples.
  • Oharano Shrine: An elegant, ancient shrine in a forest setting, historically linked to the aristocratic Fujiwara family — serene and atmospheric.
  • Shoji-ji (the “Flower Temple”): Associated with the famous poet-priest Saigyo and known for its blossoms.
  • Jojakko-ji & the Sagano fringe: On the northern edge of the western hills near Arashiyama, hillside temples with moss, thatched gates, and views.

These can be linked into a quiet temple-hopping day on foot and by occasional bus.

Hiking & Bamboo

Nishiyama is laced with gentle hiking trails through bamboo groves, satoyama farmland, and forested ridges. The area around Rakusai and the western hills offers walking routes connecting temples and viewpoints — peaceful, green, and a world away from the city. Western Kyoto is also a major bamboo-producing area, so you’ll pass working groves and bamboo craft. This is countryside walking, not wilderness — pleasant rather than strenuous — but wear proper shoes and check bus times, which are sparser than in central Kyoto.

Best Season

  • Autumn (mid–late November): The standout — Yoshimine-dera and Komyo-ji’s maple corridor rival the famous eastern temples with a fraction of the crowds.
  • Spring: Fresh green, blossoms at Shoji-ji and elsewhere.
  • Any season: The quiet is the constant draw; even in peak periods Nishiyama stays calm.

Who It’s For

Nishiyama suits travellers who:

  • Have already seen the major sights and want something different.
  • Value quiet and scenery over famous “must-see” boxes.
  • Don’t mind less frequent transport and a little walking.

If that’s you, it’s one of Kyoto’s most satisfying half-days.

Combining Your Visit

  • Arashiyama: The northern Nishiyama temples (Jojakko-ji area) connect naturally to the Arashiyama district — combine quiet western temples with the famous bamboo grove.
  • Katsura area: The renowned Katsura Imperial Villa (advance reservation) lies on the way out to western Kyoto.
  • For more green escapes, see our Kyoto nature guide and hidden gems guide .

Getting There

  • Yoshimine-dera: Bus from Hankyu Higashi-muko / JR Mukomachi stations toward the western hills (limited frequency — check the timetable both ways).
  • Komyo-ji / Oharano: Bus from Hankyu Nagaokakyo or Rakusai area.
  • Western Kyoto is best done with a planned bus schedule in hand, or as part of a guided/cycling outing.
