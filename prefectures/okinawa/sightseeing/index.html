📍 Itoman, Okinawa ★ 4.7

Himeyuri Peace Memorial

A deeply moving memorial to the 222 student nurses who perished in the Battle of Okinawa — one of the most powerful WWII sites in Japan, with an underground shelter where many died in the final days of the battle.

History WWII Memorial

📍 Shuri, Naha, Okinawa ★ 4.6

Shuri Castle

The spiritual heart of the former Ryukyu Kingdom — a UNESCO World Heritage castle on a hill above Naha, rebuilt after WWII damage and currently undergoing full restoration after a 2019 fire. The vivid red-lacquered architecture is unlike any other in Japan.

UNESCO Castle Ryukyu Kingdom

📍 Onna, Okinawa ★ 4.5

Ryukyu Mura Cultural Village

A living museum of Ryukyu culture featuring relocated traditional houses, craft workshops, and daily Eisa drum dance performances — the best place to experience authentic Okinawan folk culture in a single afternoon.

Culture Traditional Eisa Dance

⛩️
📍 Nakijin, Okinawa ★ 4.5

Kouri Island & Bridge

A tiny island reached by a stunning 2 km bridge soaring over impossibly blue water — home to white beaches, sea views and the romantic heart-shaped 'Heart Rock'.

Bridge Beach Ocean View Heart Rock
⛩️
📍 Chuo, Naha, Okinawa ★ 4.4

Kokusai-dori (International Street)

Naha's 1.6 km main shopping street buzzing with souvenir shops, shisa lion statues, sanshin music, and Okinawan food stalls. The side alleys (Ichiba Hondori) hide traditional public markets and izakaya pubs.

Shopping Street Food Naha

📍 Onna, Okinawa ★ 4.4

Cape Manzamo

An iconic elephant-trunk-shaped coral cliff on Okinawa's west coast, rising above turquoise seas — a spectacular sunset viewpoint and one of the island's most photographed spots.

Cliff Ocean View Photo Spot Free
⛩️
📍 Nakagusuku, Okinawa ★ 4.4

Nakagusuku Castle Ruins

A beautifully preserved UNESCO gusuku (Ryukyu castle) with elegant curved limestone walls and sweeping views over both the Pacific and East China Sea coasts.

World Heritage Gusuku Ruins Ocean View
⛩️
📍 Nakijin, Okinawa ★ 4.4

Nakijin Castle Ruins

A grand UNESCO castle ruin in northern Okinawa, its long undulating walls climbing a hillside — famous for Japan's earliest cherry blossoms (late January) and night illuminations.

World Heritage Gusuku Cherry Blossoms Ruins
⛩️
📍 Yomitan, Okinawa ★ 4.4

Ryukyu Kingdom Gusuku Ruins (UNESCO)

Okinawa contains nine UNESCO World Heritage Gusuku (castle) ruins and related properties — Nakijin, Zakimi, Katsuren, and Nakagusuku are the most accessible, offering dramatic views and 600-year-old coral limestone walls.

UNESCO Ruins History

⛩️
📍 Nanjo, Okinawa ★ 4.3

Sefa-utaki Sacred Site

The holiest sacred site of the Ryukyu Kingdom, a UNESCO World Heritage forest of rock formations and prayer spaces where the kingdom's high priestesses once worshipped.

World Heritage Sacred Site Forest Ryukyu
⛩️
📍 Uruma, Okinawa ★ 4.3

Katsuren Castle Ruins

A hilltop UNESCO gusuku with 360° panoramas over the sea and the Kaichu Road causeway — once home to the powerful lord Amawari, and famed for Roman coins found here.

World Heritage Gusuku Ocean View Free Entry
